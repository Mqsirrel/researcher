# Sensory Claim Verification & AMY1 Enzymology Audit: Human Evidence in Gestational Amylophagia

## 1. Verdict
**DIRECT HUMAN EVIDENCE CONFIRMS ORGANOLEPTIC TEXTURE/SMELL APPEAL; AMY1 ENZYMOLOGY IS CONTRADICTED ON RAW STARCH**

* **Sensory Model:** DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by qualitative and clinical human data. Women with amylophagy explicitly attribute their behavior to the overwhelming organoleptic appeal of raw starch—specifically its **dry, powdery mouthfeel, melting texture, and earthy aroma** (PMID: 24784797, Edwards 1959).
* **AMY1 Connection:** CONTRADICTED on enzymological grounds. Primary kinetic literature (PMID: 42480491, Butterworth 2011) confirms that native, ungelatinized starch granules require hours of incubation for fractional hydrolysis. Salivary $\alpha$-amylase is functionally silent on dry crystalline starch during a 15–30 second oral chew, meaning *AMY1* copy-number variation cannot drive the oral sensory experience of raw starch.

## 3. Indirect Evidence & Dysgeusia Link

1. **Cross-Subtype Textural Uniformity:** Amylophagy, geophagy (clay), and chalk pica share an identical physical profile: dry, bland, non-sweet, micro-particulate solids. In qualitative studies, patients describe identical somatosensory lures across all three substances (Young 2011 *Craving Earth*).
2. **Pregnancy Dysgeusia (PMID: 36618851):** Hormonal alterations in pregnancy blunt sweet/salty sensitivity and provoke metallic dysgeusia. Dry, powdery non-food starches coat the oral mucosa and absorb saliva, temporarily masking foul oral tastes without triggering odor-induced nausea.

---

## 4. Evidence Against the Pure Sensory Model

1. **Compulsive Distress:** Some gravidae describe amylophagia as an uncontrollable, distressful addiction-like urge rather than a voluntary hedonic snack. Pure sensory enjoyment does not fully explain the compulsive intensity of the craving.
2. **Subtype Selectivity:** While textures are similar, many women selectively crave *only* raw cornstarch and refuse clay, or crave *only* clay and refuse starch, suggesting substance-specific neurochemical or olfactory priming.

---

## 5. AMY1 Enzymology Check: Primary Literature Verification

Primary starch kinetic literature confirms that human salivary $\alpha$-amylase **cannot** rapidly cleave native raw starch in the mouth:

* **PMID: 42480491 (2026 Kinetic Evaluation):** Native (uncooked) starch-degrading $\alpha$-amylases achieve only **$4\%$ to $47\%$ total hydrolysis after 24 HOURS of continuous incubation at $40^\circ\text{C}$**.
* **Butterworth et al. 2011 (*Biochem J*):** Native A-type (cereal/corn) and B-type (tuber/cassava) starch granules are tightly packed crystalline structures with water excluded from the interior. Salivary amylase cannot penetrate the granule surface without prior thermal gelatinization ($>65^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Oral Reality:** During a 15–30 second chew, salivary $\alpha$-amylase hydrolyzes **$<0.1–0.5\%$ of raw starch granules**. Therefore, whether an individual has 2 copies or 14 copies of *AMY1*, the rate of glucose/maltose liberation in the mouth from dry raw cornstarch is indistinguishable from zero.
