# Sensory Model Audit: Oral Somatosensory Reinforcement & Enzymatic Constraints in Gestational Amylophagia

## 1. Verdict
**SENSORY REINFORCEMENT IS PLAUSIBLE BUT AMY1-INDEPENDENT; H-008 IS WEAKENED**

*Rationale:* Gestational amylophagy is plausible as an oral somatosensory craving for dry, powdery, particulate textures (countering pregnancy dysgeusia, nausea, and metallic mouth sensations). However, H-008's *AMY1* connection is enzymatically incompatible with raw starch: salivary $\alpha$-amylase hydrolyzes native, ungelatinized crystalline starch granules at $<1\%$ the rate of cooked starch. During a 15–30 second chew, *AMY1* copy number cannot materially alter the immediate oral sensory experience of dry raw flour or cornstarch.

---

## 2. Strongest Evidence for the Sensory Model
1. **Pregnancy Dysgeusia & Textural Salience (PMID: 36618851):** Pregnancy hormones alter taste thresholds and provoke metallic taste/nausea. Dry, bland, powdery substances absorb saliva and neutralize noxious mouth sensations without triggering food-odor aversions.
2. **Textural Overlap Across Pica Subtypes:** Qualitative ethnographies (Young 2011, Horner 1991) document identical sensory lures across amylophagy, geophagy (clay), and chalk pica: *"dry, powdery crunch that melts."*
3. **Maltooligosaccharide Taste (PMID: 30684587):** Unhydrolyzed raw crystalline starch powder is perceived by non-pica individuals as a drying, chalky irritant, whereas pica gravidae find the particle texture rewarding.

---

## 3. The Enzymatic Contradiction for H-008

| Starch State | Gelatinization & Enzymatic Hydrolysis Rate | *AMY1* Genotype Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cooked Starch (Bread, Rice, Porridge)** | Granules disrupted; rapid salivary cleavage ($<15\text{ s}$) releasing maltose (PMID: 22523588) | **HIGH:** *AMY1* directly dictates oral viscosity and glycemic response. |
| **Raw Starch (Dry Cornstarch, Raw Flour)** | Granules intact and crystalline; hydrolysis rate $<1\%$ of cooked starch | **ZERO:** Salivary amylase is functionally silent during a brief 15–30 second oral chew. |
